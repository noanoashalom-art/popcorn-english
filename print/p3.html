Walking and Texting

הליכה וכתיבת הודעות

1 A new study by researchers in Israel shows that young people are more easily distracted when they walk and use their phones at the same time. The researchers watched college students walking in the city. They found that the students made more mistakes and walked more slowly when they wrote text messages while walking. Young people were more likely to do this than older people.

2 The study was paid for by the city’s road safety department. Each of the 60 college students in the study was asked to walk the same 500-meter route six times, with a researcher walking behind them. In two of the walks, the students wrote text messages on their phones. Two walks were done while the students talked on the phone, and two walks were done without a phone at all.

3 During the two texting walks, almost all of the students – 95% – made four walking mistakes on average. These ranged from walking slowly and stopping suddenly, to crossing the street without looking, walking into other people, and missing the green light. About one-quarter of the students (25%) needed the researcher to stop them quickly to prevent an accident. Even when walking without a phone, 40% of the students made at least one mistake. When the students talked on the phone, however, the number of mistakes was 30% lower than when they texted.

4 One of the researchers explained that “most young people look at their phones more than 100 times a day, even when they walk in busy streets.” When people read or write a message, their eyes look down at the screen and not at the road. Their minds also move from paying attention to the street to thinking about the message. When this happens, they do not see cars, bicycles, or other people in time, and accidents are more likely.
