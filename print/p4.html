How Good Coaches Help Players Improve

איך מאמנים טובים עוזרים לשחקנים להשתפר

1 Coaches often tell players if they played well or badly. But a good coach does more than say “good” or “bad.” A good coach helps players understand what they did well and what they need to change. Advice from a coach can be a short comment, a demonstration, or a question. When it is clear and friendly, players are more willing to try again, even after they lose a game.

2 Imagine two young basketball players, Tamar and Amir. After a game, Tamar’s coach says only, “You played badly today.” She feels sad and does not know what to change. Amir’s coach also tells him that the game was difficult, but he adds, “Your passes were excellent. Next time, look up before you shoot.” Amir knows what to practice. This example shows why a general comment is not always enough.

3 Good advice is specific. Instead of saying only “Well done,” a coach can say, “Your defense was strong because you moved your feet quickly.” This tells the player which skill worked. When something needs to change, coaches can also give one small, practical tip: “Bend your knees a little more.” The player can then try the tip in the next practice.

4 However, giving too much advice at once can be a problem. A player who hears ten corrections after one game may feel that nothing is good enough. Coaches can choose one or two important points first. For example, with a new player, they might focus on running and passing before they talk about difficult moves. Later, the player can work on more details. This makes progress feel possible.

5 The right time for advice depends on the situation. During a game, players are busy and under pressure. If the coach shouts after every mistake, the player may lose confidence. The coach can watch, write down a few points, and talk about them after the game. But during a training exercise, a quick correction may help a player do the movement correctly right away.

6 Advice can also come from teammates. In one team, players watch short videos of their games together. Each player gives one positive comment and asks one question. For example, “Your shot was fast, but why did you pass to the left?” The player can use the question to think about better choices. Before players give advice to each other, the coach needs to teach them how to be respectful and kind.

7 Some coaches ask players to check their own performance after a game. They may use a short list: “Did I listen to the coach? Did I help my team? Did I try my best?” This is called self-evaluation. It helps players notice their strong points and weak points. It also teaches them that they are responsible for their own progress, not only the coach.

8 Advice is especially useful when players have time to use it. After a game, a coach can give the team fifteen minutes to practice one skill. Players try the skill again and notice the difference. If coaches only give advice and move on to the next game, players may never use it. Good advice should lead to more practice.

9 Players do not all feel the same about comments. Some players enjoy hearing praise in front of the whole team, while others feel embarrassed when the coach talks about their mistakes in public. Coaches can ask players how they prefer to receive advice. A private conversation or a short message may work better for a shy player. The goal is to help every player improve without feeling ashamed.

10 Good coaching is not about finding every mistake or winning every game. It is about showing players a clear next step and helping them believe they can take it. When coaches combine encouragement with practical advice, players become more confident and independent. They begin to see mistakes as part of learning, not as a reason to give up.
